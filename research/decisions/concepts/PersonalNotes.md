
5. PROTOTYPE IMPROVEMENTS TO EXPLORE
------------------------------------------------------------
A. Patient education and early detection
- Explain screening, test results and next steps in plain language.
- Address common fears and questions without promising a diagnosis.
- Explore how prevention and early detection fit the patient journey.

B. Screening and follow-up coordination
- Offer explicitly declined, unused screening slots to the next eligible
  resident under the approved allocation rules.
- Help staff identify suitable centers that provide screening, using
  verified service and availability information.
- Keep the next action and responsible staff member visible.

C. Patient information and monitoring
- Explore recording patient-reported blood pressure (BP) and other logs.
- Clearly distinguish patient-entered information from verified results.
- Explore how verified laboratory results could inform clinical review.
- Separately assess whether laboratory data belongs in a future model;
  do not assume adding it automatically improves predictive accuracy.

D. Different patient journeys
- Distinguish people without a confirmed diagnosis from people already
  diagnosed with CKD, based on documented clinical information.
- Tailor explanations and next-step displays to those different needs.
- Consider low / medium / high priority labels only after defining their
  purpose and validating the criteria; do not present them as diagnoses.


6. LGU / COMMUNITY VIEW
------------------------------------------------------------
Ideas to explore
- Show counts of residents with recorded hypertension, diabetes and
  malnutrition, with clear definitions and reporting periods.
- Highlight areas needing screening or follow-up attention.
- Distinguish recorded conditions, missing data and model estimates;
  do not label a modeled priority map as confirmed CKD prevalence.


7. NEXT STEPS AFTER SCREENING
------------------------------------------------------------
1. Confirm the actual screening, referral and follow-up workflow.
2. Validate patient barriers through patient and staff interviews.
3. Verify local screening services and the hospital-to-health-center
   information flow.
4. Review patient-facing explanations with a clinician.
5. Prioritize prototype changes based on the confirmed workflow needs.

# Heat is not part of a person's risk level

Municipal heat no longer feeds the model that sets each person's Low/Medium/High risk level. The CKDu mechanism is personal: heavy work in the heat without enough water. A town's temperature applies equally to office workers and field workers. No study gives a usable effect size (Flouris 2018 reports prevalence only), and CKDu has competing explanations. Putting heat in every resident's score would also make the analyst heatmap show hot towns as risky only because we assumed it. Heat stays where its mechanism acts: the "heavy outdoor work" item (effect tagged assumed), the citizen heat advisory card, and a separate "context only" climate panel for analysts.

## Considered options

- **Keep municipal heat in the model with an assumed effect.** This is what v2 did. Rejected: the effect size is invented and the heatmap becomes circular.
- **Drop heat entirely.** Rejected: heat stress is the leading CKDu hypothesis, and the advisory card gives useful hydration advice.

## Consequences

- Removing heat changes every model number, so it happens during tonight's retrain. Putting heat back later means re-validating and re-cutting the levels.
- The heat advisory card must say "If your doctor told you to limit fluids, follow your doctor": people with heart failure or advanced CKD are often on fluid limits.

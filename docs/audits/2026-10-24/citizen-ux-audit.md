## Scope and evidence

This is a citizen-facing interface and experience audit, not an audit of the field, coordinator, or analyst views. I compared the running app with the [PRD v3](../../AGAP-CKD-PRD-v3.md), [screen content plan](../../AGAP-CKD-Screen-Content-Plan.md), [glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md), [CitizenView](../../../frontend/src/views/CitizenView.tsx), [shared shell](../../../frontend/src/App.tsx), and [evaluation API](../../../api/main.py). I walked the live app in Playwright at **320 × 568**, **390 × 844**, and **1280 × 800** CSS pixels; completed an under-18 route and an adult Medium route; inspected the accessibility tree and computed layout; ran the Impeccable detector against the local page; and ran `npm --prefix frontend run build` (passed). This was browser emulation, not a physical-device or user-research study. No clinical judgment is made here: the PRD and content plan are the comparison baselines, and medical wording still needs the team's clinical review.

**Evidence key:** **Observed** = reproduced in the browser; **Code** = present or absent in implementation; **Specified** = required by product documents but missing from this branch. Screenshots are linked below. The prior [requirements coverage report](requirements-coverage.md) catalogues feature completeness across all channels; this document focuses on the experience of a citizen using the current interface.

## Executive diagnosis

The visual direction is approachable and the one-question rhythm is easy to follow. The experience, however, does not yet earn the trust required of a health decision aid. The central problem is a **mismatch between what the screen claims, what the person actually answered, and what the result recommends**. The app says there are 12 questions while exposing seven steps, silently supplies several health answers, gives a generic date to all result types, and presents simulated facility fixtures as real places offering free tests. Critical safety and consent branches are folded into an ordinary questionnaire. On a real phone-width viewport, a desktop demonstration shell and simulated phone frame push the primary action below the fold and clip navigation.

**36 findings:** 7 P0, 17 P1, 10 P2, and 2 P3. The severity reflects citizen impact and release risk, not a claim that all 36 are independent engineering tickets.

### Decision summary

| Priority | Decision needed before citizen-facing release |
| --- | --- |
| **P0** | Add informed consent and a safe decline path before collecting health answers. |
| **P0** | Remove hidden prefilled health claims and complete the required questions or clearly label this as a nonfunctional prototype. |
| **P0** | Make next-step advice, dates, and facility claims state-specific and verifiable. |
| **P0** | Give acute signs, pregnancy, minors, and previously diagnosed people safe routes that do not look like risk results. |
| **P1** | Replace the desktop demo frame with a responsive citizen surface; repair accessibility and failure states. |

## Journey and information architecture

| Intended citizen stage | Current experience | UX consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learn → invitation | One intro collapses education and invitation. | The citizen gets no clear explanation of what the check can and cannot tell them, then no “Not now” choice. |
| Consent | No consent decision; Start opens safety question. | The app asks sensitive health information without an explicit affirmative action or retention explanation. |
| Safety triage | One long, mixed yes/no prompt. | A person must reason across age, pregnancy, and several symptoms, with no uncertain answer or tailored next step. |
| Questions | Seven fixed screens, with several hidden defaults and missing topics. | The user cannot know what the result is actually based on. |
| Result | One shared layout for Low/Medium/High/Doctor Referral. | Urgent care, testing, and routine re-check messages inherit the same cards, date, and facility CTA. |
| Where to go | Expand-in-place list of three fixture rows. | The citizen cannot assess actual availability, reach a facility, or change town. |
| Return / recover | Start over only; no saved answer review, resume, error, or next-check reminder. | Returning users repeat a misleading default questionnaire and have no reliable recovery from interruption. |

## Cross-cutting design assessment

### Interaction and decision making

The app is strongest when it asks one plain question with large full-width answers. That rhythm should stay. The answer model needs a deliberate distinction between **unanswered**, **selected**, **unknown**, **not measured**, and **not applicable**. Current default state blurs those concepts. Auto-advance is useful for simple options, but the selected state must still be visible and understandable when returning to a question. The last-step submit needs explicit progress feedback and protection against double submission.

### Content and emotional tone

The warm green visual language and “not a diagnosis” disclaimer are useful foundations. The tone breaks at result: English API sentences, broad “screening” language, a generic date, and a care referral wrapped as a risk badge. The result should answer, in order: **What does this mean? What should I do? When? Where? Why?** The plan already defines a hierarchy for these. The words “free,” “verified,” “soon,” and “risk” need specific governance because they change real-world behavior.

### Inclusive and accessible use

The main tap targets are generally generous, and a native browser focus ring was visible on the shared nav. The critical access gaps are the unnamed icon Back button, unassociated age label, visual-only selected state, green CTA contrast, 11–12 px consequential copy, incorrect page language, and no focus announcement after screen transitions. The 320 px overflow and cut-off channel tabs add motor and comprehension costs. No physical-device touch, screen-reader session, 200% zoom, forced-colors, or user testing was performed; these remain validation gates after changes.

### Visual design and layout

The palette and rounded surfaces make the questionnaire feel calmer than a clinical form. The excessive simulated hardware framing, full shared navigation, nested card stack, and frequent micro-labels dilute the hierarchy. The visual system should be judged after the content states are corrected: otherwise polishing cards risks making false or unverified claims more persuasive. The desktop phone mockup is acceptable for a demo presentation but is not a responsive citizen app.

### Trust and system integrity

The highest-risk pattern is **decorative certainty**: a preselected answer has a checkmark, a fixture appears as an RHU, a free test is stated as fact, and a fixed date appears precise. Each looks credible because the interface is polished. The app needs explicit provenance and uncertainty on both the answer side and the recommendation side. The existing demo banner signals simulation, but it does not cure specific facility or service claims inside the citizen flow.

## Recommended work sequence

1. **P0 · `$impeccable harden` + `$impeccable clarify`:** lock the state and content matrix for consent, safety, question branches, Low/Medium/High, diagnosed, and referral; remove fixture and unverified service claims from citizen output.
2. **P0 · `$impeccable onboard`:** build the consent and safety entry flow, including decline-to-education and immediate under-18/acute routes.
3. **P0 · `$impeccable clarify`:** complete the questionnaire and state-specific next-step/date language, with clinical/content owner review.
4. **P1 · `$impeccable adapt` + `$impeccable layout`:** make Citizen a direct responsive route and remove the shared demonstration shell from the resident viewport.
5. **P1 · `$impeccable harden`:** add loading, error, empty-facility, retry and interrupted-session states that preserve answers.
6. **P1 · `$impeccable typeset` + `$impeccable colorize`:** repair reading size, labels, focus, status semantics and contrast; validate with keyboard and screen-reader walkthroughs.
7. **P2 · `$impeccable document`:** define copy, token, component and facility-data rules so the corrected states stay coherent.
8. **Final · `$impeccable polish`:** tune spacing, card depth and decorative details only after the journey and claims are correct.

## Validation gates for the next iteration

- Walk every approved result branch with **no prefilled health answers** and confirm that the displayed reason, action, date and facility qualification are derived from the entered answers.
- Test under-18, pregnancy, acute symptom, uncertain safety, already-diagnosed, consent-declined, no-facility, and network-error routes independently.
- Recheck at 320/390 px, 200% text zoom, keyboard-only navigation and at least one screen reader; use actual touch hardware for the final mobile pass.
- Check every citizen-facing medical and privacy sentence with its designated owner; treat the current PRD as the product baseline, not as a substitute for that review.
- Re-run the Impeccable detector and a production build after implementation. The current build passed; a build pass does not verify the UX or clinical branches.

## What is working

The app has a distinct, calm color direction; readable large question headings; full-width answer targets; a visible progress indicator; Back navigation; an explicit “I don't know” option on several health questions; a not-a-diagnosis line on results; and a repeat-check action. The API already returns reason and tip fields that can support a better result. These are useful foundations once answer provenance, branching and result claims are corrected.
